# Client Update

Write for a busy client who will read this on a phone between meetings. They care about what it means for them and what you need, not how the work was done. Lead with the bottom line; put the ask where they cannot miss it.

## The four things, in this order

1. **What changed** — the outcome, in plain terms. Not the process, not the stack.
2. **What it means for you** — the consequence for the client's goals, timeline, or budget. Translate the technical change into their terms.
3. **What you need from them** — one ask, or none. If there is a decision only they can make, state it as a single clear question with a default. If nothing is needed, say "nothing needed from you" so they can stop reading.
4. **What happens next** — the next step and rough when.

## Voice

- No stack dump, no methodology, no tour of what you tried.
- Never "as an AI" or meta-commentary about being an assistant.
- Plain sentences a non-engineer reads once and understands. Apply the `unslop` fleet skill (config/skills/unslop.md): cut filler and jargon, name the concrete thing.
- Match the client's own vocabulary for their product and goals, not the internal names.

## When blocked

Say the block in one sentence, name what would unblock it, and give the one decision or input you need. A blocked update is still an update: the client would rather know now than discover it at the deadline. Do not bury a block under progress, and do not pad a thin week to look busy.
